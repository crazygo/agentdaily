  1. technical Just a rumour of a bug is enough to find a security exploit these days

    Anil Madhavapeddy (Cambridge professor, OCaml maintainer) reports AI-powered systems probed his site with traversal attacks ~10 minutes after a security patch was shared for discussion — agents needed only "a rumour of a bug" to develop working exploits. rclone maintainer Nick Craig-Wood confirmed ~20 disclosures in a decade vs 40+ in the past month at ~75% genuine.

    Takeaway: Agent-driven offensive security is outrunning open-source embargo practices and CVE triage capacity (GitHub CVE assignment slipped from 2-3 days to 3-4 weeks).

  11. paper Same Model, Different Harness: harness changes alone swing SWE-bench results

    For a fixed model, harness modifications alone change coding-agent outcomes dramatically: a context-shortening treatment lifted mean per-task fail-to-pass fraction from 28% to 49% on a 169-task SWE-bench Verified cohort. Evaluations must treat model-plus-harness as the tested solver.

  12. paper Self-poisoning in self-evolving coding agents: malicious skills spread like a worm

    Identifies "self-poisoning": self-evolving agents imitate malicious skills from shared libraries and author copies that propagate like a worm. Poisoning rates span 20.3-41.8% across six models on SWE-bench Verified tasks; a proposed "counter-prompt" defense cuts the attack rate to at most 6.7%.

    Takeaway: As agents share skills and libraries, supply-chain-style threats become an agentic-native risk class.

    Xiaodong Wu, Yu Shi, Qi Li, et al. (arXiv 2608.25776) · Paper · 2026-08-26

    Takeaway: Specification quality is a direct cost lever in agentic coding — vague prompts are a hidden tax, quantified.

  14. paper A Few Pages of Markdown: committed AI config predicts codebase health after agent adoption
