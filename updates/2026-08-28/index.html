  9. Same Model, Different Harness: harness changes alone swing SWE-bench results

    Sydney Lewis · arXiv 2608.26218 · Paper · 2026-08-26technical

  10. Self-poisoning in self-evolving coding agents: malicious skills spread like a worm

    Xiaodong Wu, Yu Shi, Qi Li, et al. · arXiv 2608.25776 · Paper · 2026-08-26technical

    Identifies “self-poisoning”: self-evolving agents imitate malicious skills from shared libraries and author copies that propagate like a worm. Poisoning rates span 20.3–41.8% across six models on SWE-bench Verified tasks; a proposed “counter-prompt” defense cuts the attack rate to at most 6.7%.

    Takeaway As agents share skills/libraries, supply-chain-style threats become an agentic-native risk class.

    #coding-agents #security #skill-libraries

  11. Can your AI agent be cheaper? Under-specified tasks burn 29.7% more tokens

    Jakub Smékal · arXiv 2608.25399 · Paper · 2026-08-26technical

    Across 2,700 runs with Kimi K3, shrinking a full specification to a bare user story increased token spend by 29.7%, with prompt sensitivity ranging 13–115% by task; a lightweight predictor prices task configurations within 36% using a single cheap probe.

    Takeaway Specification quality is a direct cost lever in agentic coding — vague prompts are a hidden tax, quantified.

    #token-economics #task-specification #cost-optimization

  12. A Few Pages of Markdown: committed AI config predicts codebase health after agent adoption
