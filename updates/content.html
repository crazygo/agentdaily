Jun 26 Simon Willison

Prompt Injection as Role Confusion

Research reveals that LLMs cannot reliably distinguish between their own privileged text and untrusted user input. Models take text style more seriously than actual content, leading to concerning jailbreaks. 'Destyling' text reduces attack success from 61% to 10%, suggesting role confusion is a key challenge for prompt injection defense.

Jun 22 Simon Willison

Building Effective AI Coding Agents for the Terminal

Research paper presents OPENDEV, an open-source, command-line coding agent written in Rust, engineered specifically for terminal-based development workflows. Addresses structural failure modes in AI coding agents that dramatically accelerate implementation speed but introduce challenges spec-driven approaches don't fully resolve.

Agent in the Loop: Flipping the Narrative

Jon Udell argues against the phrase 'human in the loop' because it cedes authority to machines. Instead, we should view it as agents joining our team. Agent-assisted development need not be a black box that takes prompts and emits features - it should be a process we invite agents into, not one we're excluded from.

AI Infrastructure Buildout and Economics

Dean W. Ball analyzes industry dynamics: (1) Frontier models cost enormous amounts to train, with only a narrow post-release window to recoup costs before competition emerges and margins compress. (2) The ongoing AI infrastructure buildout assumes a global total addressable market - $100B data centers aren't being built to serve just 100 US government-approved companies.

AI and Liability: German Ruling on Google

Bruce Schneier and Nathan Sanders discuss a German ruling that Google be held liable for errors in AI overviews.
